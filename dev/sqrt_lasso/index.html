The square-root lasso

Breaking change from the earlier sqrt_lasso

sqrt_lasso now minimizes a normalized objective, and the penalty level of the earlier function does not carry over. The earlier function minimized

\[\lVert y - \beta_0 - X\beta\rVert_2 + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^{p} \omega_j |\beta_j| ,\]

and this one minimizes

\[\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\lVert y - \beta_0\mathbf{1} - X\beta\rVert_2 + \lambda \lVert \theta \rVert_1 , \qquad n = \texttt{size}(X, 1),\]

with θ the coefficients in the coordinates of the penalty (θⱼ = sⱼβⱼ, defined below). The same estimate, with the same penalty weights, needs λ = λ_earlier/√n. A call of the earlier function with n = 400 rows and λ = 20.0 becomes sqrt_lasso(X, y, 1.0); in general

sqrt_lasso(X, y, λ_earlier / sqrt(size(X, 1)))

The noise estimate σ of the result is now ‖r‖/√n for the residuals r, the root mean square of the residuals, and not their sample standard deviation. The defaults are those of the earlier function, standardize_x = true and intercept = false. The function returns a SqrtLassoSolution.

Objective

sqrt_lasso(X, y, λ) minimizes, over β₀ and β ∈ ℝᵖ,

\[\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\lVert y - \beta_0\mathbf{1} - X\beta\rVert_2 + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^{p} s_j |\beta_j| ,\]

where β₀ = 0 unless intercept = true (the intercept is not penalized) and sⱼ = 1 unless standardize_x = true, as for lasso: with standardization sⱼ is the root mean square of the column xⱼ - x̄ⱼ, where x̄ⱼ is the mean of the column with an intercept and 0 without one. Then Σⱼ sⱼ|βⱼ| = ‖θ‖₁ for the standardized coefficients θⱼ = sⱼβⱼ. Without an intercept the scale is the uncentred root mean square of the column. A column whose centred entries are all zero (a zero column, or a constant column with an intercept) has scale 0 with standardize_x = true; with either value of standardize_x the solver keeps such a column at zero, and its coefficient is 0.

The loss has no 1/√n in ProximalBase: SqrtLeastSquaresLoss(y, X) is ‖y - Xβ‖₂, and ProximalSolvers refuses a scaled square-root loss and asks for the unscaled loss at a rescaled level. So sqrt_lasso solves, on the centred and scaled data Z (columns (xⱼ - x̄ⱼ)/sⱼ) and ỹ (y - ȳ with an intercept),

solve!(θ, SqrtLeastSquaresLoss(ỹ, Z), NormL1(λps), solver),     λps = sqrt(Float64(n)) * Float64(λ),

and returns βⱼ = θⱼ/sⱼ and β₀ = ȳ - x̄ᵀβ. The level λps is a Float64 for either element type of the data. In exact arithmetic the objective that ProximalSolvers minimizes is √n times the one above: fit.result.minimum is on that scale, and fit.result.minimum/√n is the objective of sqrt_lasso at θ. With both keywords false, X and y are not copied and the solver works on them directly.

With ỹ ≠ 0, β = 0 is a minimizer in exact arithmetic if and only if λ ≥ λmax = maxⱼ |zⱼᵀỹ|/(√n‖ỹ‖) over the columns with sⱼ > 0, with zⱼ the column j of Z: the level that sets the coefficients to zero does not depend on the scale of y. In exact arithmetic the estimate is scale-equivariant in y at a fixed λ: for c > 0 the fit to c y has the coefficients, the intercept and σ of the fit to y multiplied by c.

Inputs

  • X::Matrix{T} and y::Vector{T} with T equal to Float32 or Float64; the fit has the same T. Other array types and mixed element types throw an ArgumentError that names the supported types.
  • λ::Real, positive and finite. A λ that is zero, negative, NaN or infinite throws an ArgumentError that states this and points to lasso for λ = 0: ProximalSolvers' square-root lasso needs a positive level. The stored fit.λ is T(λ).
  • The level is formed in stages. λ is converted to Float64, and λps = sqrt(Float64(n)) * Float64(λ) is computed in Float64. If Float64(λ) or the product is not finite and positive (a BigFloat or Rational λ below the Float64 range, such as big"1e-324" with n = 100, or a Float64 λ whose product overflows, such as 1e308 with n = 40), sqrt_lasso throws its own ArgumentError, "the level √n λ for n = … and λ = … is not a finite positive Float64", and ProximalSolvers is not called. Otherwise ProximalSolvers checks that λps is representable in T (a finite positive normal or subnormal number), and its error ("not representable without overflow or underflow") is passed on unchanged. That limit is on √n λ computed in this way, not on λ: for Float32 data with n = 40, λ = 1e-47 gives λps ≈ 6.3e-47, which rounds to zero in Float32.
  • n ≥ 1, length(y) == n and p ≥ 1 are checked by this package (a DimensionMismatch). With a keyword set, a design (or, with an intercept, a response) that does not preprocess (a non-finite entry, a mean or scale that overflows) is refused with the ArgumentError of lasso, which names the column or the step and starts with lasso:. The other checks of the data (for example a non-finite response without an intercept, or any non-finite entry with both keywords false) are ProximalSolvers', and their errors are passed on.
  • intercept and standardize_x are true or false; any other value throws an ArgumentError that names the keyword.
  • X and y are not modified and the fit keeps no reference to them.

Certification and accuracy

Two statements are kept apart, here, in the docstring of SqrtLassoSolution and in show.

Certification of the transformed problem. fit.result.converged and fit.result.gap are ProximalSolvers' and are neither recomputed nor changed. converged = true means that the duality certificate of ProximalSolvers proved that the relative gap (P(θ) - P*)/P* of the problem it solved is at most 1e-4, and gap is that bound, rounded up in T. The problem it solved is ‖ỹ - Zθ‖ + λps ‖θ‖₁ for the stored Z and ỹ in T, at the level λps = √n λ and the point θ (fit.result.minimizer). show reads "duality certificate of the transformed problem: met" or "not met".

Accuracy in the original coordinates. This is not certified. The returned β, β₀ and residuals are formed from θ, and the objective of sqrt_lasso at them differs from the transformed problem by rounding. The sizes of the terms, for u = eps(T)/2, hold where the quantity being rounded is in the normal range of T:

  • the centred and scaled data: an absolute error of about eps(T)(|xᵢⱼ| + |x̄ⱼ|)/sⱼ in an entry of Z (and of eps(T)(|yᵢ| + |ȳ|) in an entry of ỹ), from the mean and the centring, and a relative error in the scale sⱼ of up to about n eps(T) (a sum of n squares), usually much smaller;
  • the back-map βⱼ = θⱼ/sⱼ: a relative error of eps(T)/2;
  • the intercept β₀ = ȳ - x̄ᵀβ: an error of about eps(T)(|ȳ| + Σⱼ|x̄ⱼβⱼ|), which grows with the number of columns;
  • the residuals y - β₀ - Xβ: an error of about p eps(T)(|yᵢ| + |β₀| + Σⱼ|xᵢⱼβⱼ|).

A result below floatmin(T) is rounded to a subnormal number or to zero, with an absolute error of up to nextfloat(zero(T))/2, which is a relative error of up to 1, and the sizes above do not apply (an example follows the checker below). With both keywords false the stored problem is the problem that was given, and the first three terms do not arise. For Float64 fits on inputs of moderate magnitude (a range stated in the tests), the tests of this package compare the duality gap below with 1e-4 plus a perturbation term derived from these sizes; for Float32 fits they do not make that comparison, and check the transformed problem and weak duality only.

Nothing in sqrt_lasso bounds the accuracy of β and β₀ in the original coordinates, and the package states no accuracy claim of its own. To measure it, compute a duality gap in the original coordinates, independently of the solver. Let Λ = √n λ, r = y - β₀1 - Xβ (with β₀ = 0 without an intercept) and x̃ⱼ = xⱼ - x̄ⱼ. With an intercept let r_c be r centred at its mean; without one, r_c = r. The centring is needed with an intercept: with a rounded β₀ the vector 1ᵀr is not zero. For r_c ≠ 0 let gⱼ = |x̃ⱼᵀr_c|/(sⱼ‖r_c‖) over the columns with sⱼ > 0 (g = 0 if there is none), t = min(1, Λ/maxⱼ gⱼ) and u = t r_c/‖r_c‖. Then ‖u‖ ≤ 1 and |x̃ⱼᵀu| ≤ Λ sⱼ, and with an intercept also 1ᵀu = 0. For every b, and with an intercept every b₀ (without one b₀ = 0 and x̃ⱼ = xⱼ), ‖y - b₀1 - Xb‖ + Λ Σⱼ sⱼ|bⱼ| ≥ uᵀ(y - b₀1 - Xb) + Λ Σⱼ sⱼ|bⱼ| ≥ uᵀy, by Cauchy–Schwarz, the bound on |x̃ⱼᵀu| and, with an intercept, 1ᵀu = 0. So D = uᵀy is a lower bound of the minimum P*, and P = ‖r‖ + Λ Σⱼ sⱼ|βⱼ| is an upper bound. When D > 0, 0 < D ≤ P* ≤ P and (P - D)/D bounds (P - P*)/P* for the returned point, up to the rounding of the 256-bit evaluation. When D ≤ 0, which can happen at a point that is far from the minimum (for example after one pass), (P - D)/D is not a bound of anything and can be negative, so the function below returns Inf; it also returns Inf when r_c = 0, where u is undefined. It computes the quantities in 256-bit arithmetic from the raw X and y, a high-precision evaluation and not a rigorous enclosure; it clamps the numerator P - D at zero, because that rounding can leave the computed D slightly above P at a point that is at the minimum. The level defaults to fit.λ, which is T(λ); pass the λ given to sqrt_lasso as λ when it is not a number of T.

function dual_gap(X, y, fit; λ = fit.λ, intercept = fit.hasintercept, standardize_x = true)
  setprecision(BigFloat, 256) do
    n, p = size(X)
    Xb, yb, β = BigFloat.(X), BigFloat.(y), BigFloat.(fit.coefficients)
    x̄ = intercept ? [sum(Xb[:, j]) / n for j in 1:p] : zeros(BigFloat, p)
    Xt = Xb .- transpose(x̄)
    s = standardize_x ? [sqrt(sum(abs2, Xt[:, j]) / n) for j in 1:p] : ones(BigFloat, p)
    Λ = sqrt(BigFloat(n)) * BigFloat(λ)
    r = (yb .- BigFloat(fit.intercept)) .- Xb * β
    rc = intercept ? r .- sum(r) / n : r
    N = sqrt(sum(abs2, rc))
    iszero(N) && return Inf                       # u is undefined
    P = sqrt(sum(abs2, r)) + Λ * sum(s .* abs.(β))
    g = maximum((abs(sum(Xt[:, j] .* rc)) / s[j] for j in 1:p if s[j] > 0); init = zero(BigFloat)) / N
    t = g > Λ ? Λ / g : one(BigFloat)
    D = t * sum(rc .* yb) / N
    D > 0 ? Float64(max(P - D, zero(P)) / D) : Inf   # a bound of (P - P*)/P* only where D > 0
  end
end

Where the certificate is defined

A run of ProximalSolvers' square-root coordinate descent ends with a certificate (converged = true, and gap the proved bound), without one (converged = false, gap = NaN, and a warning "stopped without a duality certificate"), or at maxpasses (converged = false, gap = NaN, and a warning "did not converge in maxpasses"). passes and the text of the warning tell the last two apart. When a run reaches the certificate, how the threshold is tightened, and the range in which the certificate is defined are those of the CDSolver documentation of ProximalSolvers (its section on the square-root lasso), which this package does not restate, change or supplement with a certificate, a stopping rule or a recovery path. Two statements of that documentation are used on this page, for a run that reaches the certificate check:

  • the certificate is undefined where the recomputed residual of the transformed problem is zero while some θⱼ ≠ 0 (the usual outcome at an exact fit y = Xβ with a positive level, where a coordinatewise minimum need not be a minimum), and where that residual is nonzero with a sum of squares below n 2⁻⁹⁶⁹ (a root mean square below about 1.4e-146 on the scale of the transformed problem): the run then ends with converged = false;
  • ProximalSolvers handles a response ỹ that is all zero, with all coefficients zero, before that test, and certifies it (converged = true, gap = 0).

For ỹ = 0, which a zero response gives and a constant response with an intercept gives (it centres to zeros), the minimizer is θ = 0, so β = 0, β₀ = ȳ and σ = 0; the second statement above applies to it. With a zero design the minimizer is β = 0 with β₀ = ȳ (β₀ = 0 without an intercept), and the residual is ỹ.

exact = sqrt_lasso(Float64[1 2; 2 0; 3 1; 6 1], 5 .+ [1, 2, 3, 6] ./ 2, 0.5,
                   CDSolver(order = :cyclic, momentum = false); intercept = true, standardize_x = false)
(coefficients = exact.coefficients, intercept = exact.intercept, σ = exact.σ,
 converged = exact.result.converged, gap = exact.result.gap, passes = exact.result.passes)
(coefficients = [0.5, 0.0], intercept = 5.0, σ = 0.0, converged = false, gap = NaN, passes = 3)

This fit is exact (y = 5 + x₁/2), the residuals are zero, and ProximalSolvers stops without a certificate: with a zero residual the dual direction is undefined. It logs its warning.

Float32

At the default tol = 1e-7 the solver does not tighten the threshold of the certificate below the floor 16 eps(Float32)‖ỹ‖ ≈ 1.9e-6‖ỹ‖ for Float32 data (the candidate tol/100 is refused below it), so a run whose first certificate attempt fails ends without a certificate instead of continuing at a smaller threshold. The rounding of Float32 coefficients can also keep the largest change of each full pass above the threshold, and the run then ends at maxpasses. In both cases converged is false. The tests of this package set tol = 1e-6 and maxpasses = 2000 for Float32 data, and accept a certified result, checked independently for the transformed problem, or a run that reports itself not converged. Read fit.result.converged: a Float32 fit that is not converged is not certified, and a converged one is certified for the transformed problem, not for the original coordinates.

The noise estimate

σ = ‖r‖/√n for the residuals r = y - β₀ - Xβ on the original data, computed as the root mean square of the entries divided by a power of two near the largest, so that it does not overflow, with σ = 0 when all residuals are zero. It is the noise estimate of the scaled lasso at the returned coefficients, a residual-based estimate whose finite-sample bias can have either sign. It tends to fall below the noise level where the fit adapts to y, because the residuals of a fit that follows the data are smaller than the errors. It can exceed the noise level where the fit leaves signal in the residuals: for example at β = 0 (a level above λmax) with a signal present, σ is the root mean square of y - β₀ and includes the signal. No degrees-of-freedom correction is applied. The earlier function returned the sample standard deviation of the residuals.

A pivotal level

A penalty level of the square-root lasso can be chosen without the noise level. For Gaussian errors, Belloni, Chernozhukov and Wang take, for columns scaled to (1/n)Σᵢ xᵢⱼ² = 1,

\[\lambda = c\,\Phi^{-1}\!\left(1 - \frac{\alpha}{2p}\right)\Big/\sqrt{n}\]

in the normalized objective of this page, with c > 1 (for example c = 1.1) and a small α (for example α = 0.05); Φ⁻¹ is the standard normal quantile function. With standardize_x = true and without an intercept the columns are scaled to that mean square, and with an intercept to unit variance. For α = 0.05 and p = 50, 1 - α/(2p) = 0.9995, and Φ⁻¹(0.9995) ≈ 3.2905267: the standard normal tail beyond 3.2905267 is about 0.0005, so the two-sided tail is about 0.001 = α/p. The value is hard-coded here, with no dependency on a package for the quantile.

rng = Xoshiro(2)
n, p = 100, 50
X = randn(rng, n, p)
σ_true = 0.5
y = X[:, 1:3] * [2.0, -1.5, 1.0] + σ_true * randn(rng, n)

quantile = 3.2905267                          # Φ⁻¹(1 - 0.05 / (2 * 50)) = Φ⁻¹(0.9995)
λ = 1.1 * quantile / sqrt(n)
fit = sqrt_lasso(X, y, λ, CDSolver(order = :cyclic, momentum = false); intercept = true)
(λ = λ, selected = findall(!iszero, fit.coefficients), σ_hat = fit.σ, σ_true = σ_true,
 converged = fit.result.converged)
(λ = 0.361957937, selected = [1, 2, 3], σ_hat = 0.5003215192498334, σ_true = 0.5, converged = true)

The level λ was chosen without σ_true; σ_hat = fit.σ is the estimate of the noise level from the fit.

HighDimensionalModels.sqrt_lasso — Function
sqrt_lasso(X, y, λ, solver = CDSolver(); intercept = false, standardize_x = true) -> SqrtLassoSolution

Breaking. The earlier sqrt_lasso minimized ‖y - β₀ - Xβ‖ + λ Σⱼ ωⱼ|βⱼ|. This one minimizes ‖y - β₀1 - Xβ‖/√n + λ‖θ‖₁, so the same estimate needs λ = λ_earlier/√n: an earlier sqrt_lasso(X, y, 20.0) on n = 400 rows is now sqrt_lasso(X, y, 1.0). The noise estimate σ of the result is now ‖r‖/√n for the residuals r, not their sample standard deviation. The defaults standardize_x = true and intercept = false are those of the earlier function.

Minimize ‖y - β₀1 - Xβ‖/√n + λ Σⱼ sⱼ|βⱼ| over β₀ and β, with n = size(X, 1) and ‖⋅‖ the Euclidean norm, by coordinate descent. X must be a Matrix{T} and y a Vector{T}, with T equal to Float32 or Float64, and λ a Real; solver is a CDSolver. Other argument types throw an ArgumentError, and nothing is converted. X and y are not modified, and the solution keeps no reference to them. β₀ is 0 and unpenalized unless intercept = true. The weights sⱼ are those of lasso: sⱼ = 1 with standardize_x = false, and with standardize_x = true the root mean square of the column xⱼ - x̄ⱼ (the centred column with an intercept, the column itself without one), so that Σⱼ sⱼ|βⱼ| = ‖θ‖₁ for the standardized coefficients θⱼ = sⱼβⱼ. Without an intercept the scale is the uncentred root mean square. With standardize_x = true a column whose centred entries are all zero (a zero column, or a constant column with an intercept) has scale 0; with either value of standardize_x the solver keeps such a column at zero, and its coefficient is 0.

λ must be positive and finite: ProximalSolvers' square-root lasso needs a positive level, and a λ that is zero, negative, NaN or infinite throws an ArgumentError (for λ = 0, lasso fits the least squares problem). Because ProximalBase.SqrtLeastSquaresLoss is the unnormalized ‖y - Xβ‖ and ProximalSolvers refuses a scaled square-root loss, the solve is solve!(θ, SqrtLeastSquaresLoss(ỹ, Z), NormL1(λps), solver) on the data of lasso: Z has the columns (xⱼ - x̄ⱼ)/sⱼ (xⱼ - x̄ⱼ without standardization) and ỹ is y - ȳ with an intercept (X and y themselves, not copied, with both keywords false). Then βⱼ = θⱼ/sⱼ and β₀ = ȳ - x̄ᵀβ, with the helpers and the error messages of lasso for a design or response that does not preprocess (a message that starts with lasso: and names the column or the step).

The level is formed in stages. λ is converted to Float64, and λps = sqrt(Float64(n)) * Float64(λ) is computed in Float64, a Float64 level for either element type. If Float64(λ) or the product is not finite and positive (a BigFloat or Rational λ below the Float64 range, or a Float64 λ whose product overflows), sqrt_lasso throws its own ArgumentError, "the level √n λ … is not a finite positive Float64", and ProximalSolvers is not called. Otherwise ProximalSolvers checks that λps is representable in T without overflow or underflow, and its error is passed on unchanged: that limit is on √n λ computed in this way, not on λ.

For ỹ = 0 (a zero y, or a constant y with an intercept, which centres to zeros) the minimizer is θ = 0 for each λ > 0: β = 0, β₀ = ȳ and σ = 0. ProximalSolvers handles an all-zero response with all coefficients zero before its test of the size of the residual and certifies it (converged = true, gap = 0) at a full pass that its check reaches; whether a run reaches that check depends on the solver settings (see the CDSolver documentation of ProximalSolvers). For a run that reaches the check, the certificate is undefined for a nonzero residual whose root mean square is below about 1.4e-146 on the scale of the transformed problem, and the run then ends with converged = false.

n ≥ 1, length(y) == n and size(X, 2) ≥ 1 are checked here (a DimensionMismatch); the other checks (finite data, the level) are those of ProximalBase and ProximalSolvers or of the preprocessing of lasso. A keyword that is not true or false throws an ArgumentError.

The convergence report is ProximalSolvers' (fit.result.converged, gap, passes, tolerance, minimum), for the problem in the coordinates θ; see SqrtLassoSolution for what it certifies and for what it does not. An uncertified stop (for example at an exact fit, where the residual is zero) and a stop at maxpasses both give converged == false and gap == NaN, with a warning each; passes and the text of the warning tell them apart. This package adds no certificate, stopping rule or recovery to the solver's. With CDSolver() the coordinate order is random; CDSolver(order = :cyclic, momentum = false) fixes it.

σ = ‖r‖/√n is the root mean square of the residuals on the original data, the noise estimate of the scaled lasso at the returned coefficients. It is a residual-based estimate whose finite-sample bias can have either sign: it tends to fall below the noise level where the fit adapts to y, and it can exceed it where the fit leaves signal in the residuals (for example β = 0 at a level above λmax with a signal present).

SqrtLassoSolution{T, R}

The fit returned by sqrt_lasso, with fields

  • coefficients::Vector{T}: the coefficients β, one per column of the design;
  • intercept::T: the intercept β₀, which is 0 when hasintercept is false;
  • hasintercept::Bool: whether sqrt_lasso was called with intercept = true;
  • residuals::Vector{T}: y - β₀ - X * coefficients on the data given to sqrt_lasso, computed once at the returned point;
  • λ::T: the penalty level of sqrt_lasso (the normalized one), converted to T;
  • σ::T: the noise estimate ‖r‖/√n for the residuals r above, the root mean square of the residuals (0 when all residuals are zero), computed from the entries divided by a power of two not above the largest |rᵢ|, so that the squares in the sum are at most 4;
  • result::R: the ProximalSolvers result, unchanged. For sqrt_lasso it is a ProximalSolvers.CDResults.

Two statements are kept apart, because they have different strengths.

Certification of the transformed problem. result.converged and result.gap are those of ProximalSolvers and are not recomputed or changed here. result.converged is true when ProximalSolvers' duality certificate proved that the relative gap of the problem it solved is at most 1e-4, and false otherwise; result.gap is that bound (rounded up in T) where it is true. The problem it solved is ‖ỹ - Zθ‖ + λps ‖θ‖₁ for the stored matrix Z and vector ỹ in T (the centred and scaled data of sqrt_lasso, or X and y themselves with both keywords false), at the level λps = √n λ of sqrt_lasso and the point θ. In exact arithmetic its objective is √n times the objective of sqrt_lasso. With result.converged == false the point is not certified, whether the run ended at maxpasses or without a certificate (the conditions are those of the CDSolver documentation of ProximalSolvers); result.passes and the warning that ProximalSolvers logs tell the two apart.

Accuracy in the original coordinates. This is not certified. The returned β, β₀ and residuals are formed from θ by the back-transformation, and they differ from the transformed problem by the rounding of the preprocessing (an absolute error of about eps(T)(|xᵢⱼ| + |x̄ⱼ|)/sⱼ in an entry of Z, and a relative error of up to about n eps(T) in a scale sⱼ), of βⱼ = θⱼ/sⱼ (a relative error of eps(T)/2), of β₀ (an error of about eps(T)(|ȳ| + Σⱼ|x̄ⱼβⱼ|), which grows with the number of columns) and of the residuals. These sizes hold where the quantity being rounded is in the normal range of T. A result below floatmin(T) is rounded to a subnormal number or to zero, with an absolute error of up to nextfloat(zero(T))/2, which is a relative error of up to 1: for example, with Float64 data a coefficient θⱼ/sⱼ ≈ 7e-401 is stored as 0 although θⱼ ≈ 7e-101 is certified for the transformed problem, and the objective of the stored β can then be far from the certified one. The page on the square-root lasso gives a duality gap computed in the original coordinates, which a user can run on a fit.

result is documented on the scale of ProximalSolvers: result.minimizer is θ (the same vector as coefficients unless standardize_x = true), result.minimum is √n times the objective of sqrt_lasso in exact arithmetic, result.tolerance is the change threshold, in units of tol‖ỹ‖ with the default relative = true and ỹ ≠ 0, at the end of the run (it is divided by 100 when the certificate fails and the run continues), and result.gap is a relative gap, which has no scale.

The positional constructor SqrtLassoSolution(coefficients, intercept, hasintercept, residuals, λ, σ, result) is internal; build a fit with sqrt_lasso.
